Hands & Travel

Use your bare hand, seen by your laptop webcam, to reach far objects with Go-Go, grab and move them, then get around the world by leaning and teleporting. Everything is already wired up. You write seven short pieces of code, most of them one or two lines.

Start of Session A

Set up

You need Unity 6 (any 6000.x version), a laptop webcam, and CS428Studio2_resources.zip, which is uploaded on UIC Canvas. No VR headset this time: the laptop screen is your window into the scene.

  1. In Unity Hub, create a new project with the Universal 3D template.
  2. Open Window ▸ Package Manager, click + ▸ Install package by name, and enter:
    com.unity.ai.inference

    This is Unity's Inference Engine. It runs Google's MediaPipe hand and face models on your GPU.

  3. Download CS428Studio2_resources.zip from UIC Canvas and unzip it. Drag the CS428Studio folder inside it into your project's Assets folder. Install the package first, so Unity recognizes the three .onnx model files.
  4. From the menu bar, choose CS428 ▸ Build Studio Scene. This builds the room, the objects and the camera rig, and connects all the scripts.
  5. Press Play. Allow camera access if asked. A small mirrored webcam preview appears in the bottom-right corner, with yellow dots on your hand.
The panel at the top left says hand OK when you raise a hand, and an orange stick-figure hand appears in the scene.
Older or slower laptop?
  • On the Studio object, lower Max Tracking Hz on Hand Face Tracker from 20 to 12. Tracking runs in the background, so the scene stays smooth.
  • Set the Game view to a fixed size such as 1280×720 instead of Free Aspect.
  • Close video calls and browser tabs that use the camera or GPU.
  • No webcam, or still too slow? Press M for mouse simulation: the mouse moves the hand, scroll is reach, click is pinch, arrow keys lean. You can finish every TODO this way.
Background

How the tracking works

Every frame, a palm detector finds your hand, the image is cropped and turned upright, and a hand landmarker returns 21 joints. You do not need to touch this code. You only use three things it gives you: where the hand is, how big it looks, and how far apart your thumb and index tips are.

KeyWhat it does

Session A

Reach & grab

A1

Calibrate your reach

no code

A webcam has no depth sensor, so we use a simple trick: the closer your hand is to the camera, the bigger it looks. Two key presses tell the program what "resting" and "fully stretched" look like for you.

  1. Rest your hand in front of your chest, palm toward the screen. Press 1.
  2. Stretch your arm toward the screen. Press 2.
  3. Measure your arm from shoulder to palm with a ruler or a partner. On the Studio object, type it into Virtual Hand ▸ Arm Length (meters).
With Go-Go off (G), the panel shows real reach near 0.25 m at rest and near your arm length when stretched. The near table is easy to touch. The far table is not.
A2

Make the arm grow: Go-Go

GoGo.cs

Go-Go (Poupyrev et al., UIST 1996) keeps your virtual hand normal near your body, where you need precision. Past a threshold D, set to two-thirds of your arm, the virtual arm grows with the square of the extra distance.

Rv = Rr                    if Rr < D
Rv = Rr + k · (Rr − D)²    otherwise
TODO A2 · GoGo.Map()Replace return realReach; with the two cases above. About three lines: an if, then the formula.
With Go-Go on, stretching toward the far table sends the hand out about 3 m. The panel shows "full arm reaches … m". If that number is below 3.5, raise k in the Inspector.

Think: near full stretch, moving your real hand 1 cm moves the virtual hand much more. Why does the far hand shake more than the near hand? (Hint: the slope of the curve is 1 + 2k(Rr − D).)

A3

Grab and move: direct manipulation

HandGrabber.cs

Selection is already done for you. When the hand touches an object, the object turns yellow. Pinch (thumb tip to index tip) and it turns green. Your job is to make the grabbed object move and turn with your hand.

Grab the Red Box on the far table, rotate your wrist, and the box turns with it. Open your fingers and it falls.
A4

Play, tune, reflect

Inspector only
  1. Bring the Red Box, Blue Can and Yellow Box from the far table to the green Drop Pad on the near table. Then get the Purple Box off the pillar.
  2. Change Virtual Hand ▸ Smoothing to 0, then to 0.9. Which feels better far away? Which feels better up close?
  3. Press G to turn Go-Go off. Which objects can you still reach?

Answer on the worksheet: (1) Where does Go-Go feel precise, and where does it feel shaky? (2) What did smoothing trade away? (3) The hand's blob shadow on the floor: why does a flat screen need it?

Session B

Lean & teleport

Open last session's project and press Play. Press Tab to switch to TRAVEL mode. The hand turns blue and stays 1:1 (no Go-Go), and face tracking switches on.

B1

Lean to move

LeanTravel.cs

Your head becomes a joystick. Leaning toward the screen makes your face look bigger to the webcam, so you move forward. Leaning sideways slides your face left or right, so you turn. Sit up straight and press 3 to set your neutral pose.

Lean in a little: nothing happens (dead zone). Lean more: you glide forward. Lean right: you turn right. The panel shows speed in m/s and turn speed in °/s.
B2

Point & teleport

TeleportTravel.cs

Point your fingers where you want to go. An arc shows the landing spot: green means OK, red means too steep or an object. Pinch to go. The screen blinks to black for a moment, which hides the jump.

TODO B2a · ArcPoint()Right now the arc is a straight laser that never comes down. Add gravity: p(t) = start + velocity·t + ½·g·t², with g pointing down (Vector3.down and the gravity field).
TODO B2b · MoveRigTo()One line: the rig's origin is the floor under you, so put it on the target.
Aim at the floor ahead and pinch: you land there. Try the gray Platform on your right. Aiming at a wall turns the arc red, and pinching does nothing.
B3

Lean vs. teleport

no code

Reach the red Goal Flag about 24 m ahead, past the walls. Do it twice: first with leaning only, then with teleport only. Press R between runs.

  1. Time each run with your phone.
  2. At the flag, close your eyes and point to where you started. Then open them and check.
  3. Rate comfort from 1 (felt sick) to 5 (felt fine).

Answer on the worksheet: (1) Which was faster? (2) Which kept you better oriented? (3) Which was more comfortable, and why? (4) Which would you use in a headset, and when would you use the other?

Troubleshooting

ProblemFix
The CS428 menu doesn't appear, or the console shows errors about Unity.InferenceEngineThe package isn't installed. Install com.unity.ai.inference, then right-click the CS428Studio folder and choose Reimport.
The preview stays black or says "waiting for webcam"Close other apps that use the camera (Zoom, Teams, browser). On macOS, allow Unity Hub and Unity in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Camera, then restart Unity. With two cameras, change Webcam Index.
The hand flickers in and outAdd light in front of you, avoid a bright window behind you, and keep the whole hand in the preview. Lower Hand Score Threshold to 0.4. Still failing in good light? Untick Convert To SRGB on Hand Face Tracker.
The pinch doesn't trigger, or triggers by itselfTurn your palm toward the camera so both fingertips are visible. Adjust Pinch Start and Pinch End on Virtual Hand.
Reaching does nothingPress 1 and 2 again. Your stretched hand must look clearly bigger than your resting hand.
Leaning doesn't move youYou must be in TRAVEL mode (Tab) with your face in view. Press 3 while sitting straight.
Low frame rateLower Max Tracking Hz, set a fixed Game view size, or press M for mouse simulation.
